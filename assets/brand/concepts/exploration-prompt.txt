Use case: logo-brand.
Asset type: a single brand exploration sheet, landscape layout with three equally spaced concepts, for plansplease.
Primary request: develop three carefully constructed, memorable geometric logo symbols for a developer tool that keeps and reopens HTML pages made by coding agents. Brand character: precise and approachable. The symbols must feel custom drawn and immediately distinguishable by silhouette, without generic file icons.
Canvas: very dark charcoal #0c0c0e. Symbols: warm coral #e5795a, occasional off-white #f4f4f5 for small annotations. Flat solid shapes, vector-friendly edges, no gradients, texture, lighting or 3D.
Concept A, titled "01 / HELD": an asymmetrical upright loop that reads subtly as a lowercase p; an angular sheet-like head joined to a rounded supporting stem. Strong positive and negative shapes; one purposeful opening or corner cut creates a distinctive silhouette. Communicate a page being held.
Concept B, titled "02 / FOLD": a compact single continuous ribbon, combining a page fold and a lowercase p through clever negative space. Balanced structural angles with gently rounded outer corners. Thick bold proportions.
Concept C, titled "03 / RETURN": two offset rounded brackets interlocking around one rectangular page-like negative space, with one slight diagonal termination. A distinctive compact emblem for keeping and coming back to an artifact.
Composition: three columns with generous empty space. In each column one large standalone coral symbol, beneath it the same symbol in a coral rounded-square icon tile with dark symbol, and a smaller monochrome version. Label each concept unobtrusively. Bottom footer text verbatim: "plansplease / precise + approachable". These labels are only for this exploration sheet and are not part of the logos.
Avoid: folded document outline with horizontal lines, cloud/upload/download arrows, AI sparkle stars, chain links, conventional stock p letters, shields, ticks, mascots, browser-window diagrams, recognizable logos of existing products. Prioritize visual distinctiveness and legibility at small sizes. Do not illustrate all the tool's features.
